# Common Garment Defects and How to Review Them

A buyer can reduce avoidable garment issues by reviewing samples against a written specification, documenting comments clearly and approving the correct version before bulk production. Terms such as puckering, skewing, uneven seams or print-placement variation describe visible outcomes; the right response is to identify what the buyer sees, where it occurs and what the approved standard should be.

## Review the garment systematically

Start with the overall garment: silhouette, measurements, symmetry, seam alignment, colour and decoration. Then move through the specific construction points listed in the product brief. Compare the sample with the approved measurement table, artwork, trim references and placement notes.

Photographs can support a comment, but they should be paired with a location and instruction. For example, identify the seam, panel or print area, state the observed issue and say what must match the approved reference. Keep one version of the comment log rather than collecting inconsistent notes from separate reviewers.

## Recognise common review topics

Puckering describes unwanted rippling or gathering near a seam. Skewing describes a garment or panel that does not sit squarely as intended. Uneven seam allowances, inconsistent stitching, uneven print placement, colour variation and trim attachment are other examples of matters a buyer may need to review.

The article does not diagnose a particular cause for each outcome. Fabric, construction, decoration and finishing choices vary by product. Use the sample review to identify the expected appearance and ask the manufacturer how the specific issue should be assessed or corrected.

## Make sample approval actionable

A useful approval record includes the sample reference, product name, measurement version, fabric direction, colour reference, decoration file or placement, trims, labels and each approved or rejected point. If a change is requested, say whether it is a correction to the current sample or a new product decision.

Do not approve bulk production with unresolved comments. If a decision still depends on a buyer-provided material, artwork or measurement, record it as open and ask for confirmation in the quotation or sample workflow.

## Questions brands often ask

### Is a visual reference enough for quality approval?

No. Pair it with a written specification, clear location notes and the approved sample or measurement reference.

### What should I do if a sample differs from the brief?

Record the difference precisely, keep the approved brief attached to the comment and ask what revision or review step is needed before proceeding.

### Can quality expectations be discussed before a quote?

Yes. Include the product, quantity, material direction, fit, decoration and any critical quality points when you request a quote.
