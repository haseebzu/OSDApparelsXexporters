# Fabric Shrinkage: Questions to Ask Before Production

Fabric behaviour can affect fit, measurements and care information, so it belongs in the product brief before a garment is approved for bulk production. Brands should identify the intended fabric, the finished measurements they expect, the intended care approach and any shrinkage question that needs to be addressed during sampling. The answer for one fabric or construction should not be assumed to apply to another.

## Explain the intended finished garment

Begin with the product type, fit direction, base measurements and size range. State the intended fabric composition or direction where known, the desired weight and any stretch or surface expectation. If the brand has a target post-care measurement or tolerance requirement, include it in the written specification rather than leaving it as an informal expectation.

Care information matters because customer use can affect the way a garment performs. The buyer should determine the appropriate market-facing care information for the finished product and confirm that it is consistent with the approved garment and fabric direction.

## Use sampling to ask specific questions

A sample provides a practical point for reviewing fit and construction. If shrinkage is a concern, identify it in the sample brief and ask what information is needed to assess it for the proposed fabric and garment. Keep the sample version, measurements and buyer comments together.

Avoid a generic statement such as “pre-shrunk” unless it is supported by the documented product specification and approved wording. The same caution applies to wash, dye and finish claims. Owner-sensitive finishes in the product drafts are marked for confirmation before publication.

## Keep measurements and care decisions aligned

The size chart, sample approval record, label copy and quotation should refer to the same product version. A later material or finish change may require the buyer to revisit fit, measurements or care instructions. Record that change rather than relying on an earlier sample approval.

For a collection with several garment types, review each one on its own merits. A T-shirt, hoodie and ribbed item may have different construction and material considerations. A shared brand standard can guide the process, but it does not replace a product-specific brief.

## Questions brands often ask

### Should shrinkage be discussed before sampling?

Yes. Raise the intended fabric, fit and care expectations in the first brief so the sample review can address the relevant product questions.

### Can one sample approval cover every colour or finish?

Do not assume so. Identify any variation in fabric, colour, wash, finish or decoration and ask whether a further review is needed.

### What should I send with the enquiry?

Send the garment type, quantity, fabric direction, measurements or tech pack, colour and decoration requirements, labels and packaging needs, destination country and target date.
