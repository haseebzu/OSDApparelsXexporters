# Private-Label Labels, Hang Tags and Packaging

Labels, hang tags and packaging turn a garment into a branded product at the point a customer receives it. They work best when planned with the garment specification rather than added at the end. A useful brief explains what information must appear, where each item will sit, what the buyer will supply and which details need to be confirmed in the quote.

## Separate the jobs each item does

A neck label normally identifies the brand and may carry size information. A care label carries the information the buyer needs for the intended market. A hang tag can carry product, brand or retail information. Polybags and outer packaging protect garments during handling and fulfilment.

These items should not be treated as interchangeable. Start by listing the purpose of each item and whether it is attached to, packed with or applied around the garment. That makes it easier to brief placement, dimensions, materials and artwork.

## Prepare artwork that can be checked

Supply the current logo artwork and any required copy in an editable, legible format. Include colour references, the intended placement and the orientation. If a label has more than one side, provide the content for each side. If a hang tag uses a string, pin or other attachment method, describe that requirement in the brief.

The buyer remains responsible for confirming that all brand, care, fibre and market information is appropriate for the product and destination. Requirements can differ by market, so check the relevant official guidance before approving final label copy.

## Define label placement early

A placement note avoids ambiguity. Identify the garment area, the distance from a seam or edge where relevant, and whether the label is woven, printed or another approved format. Include a reference photograph or technical drawing when placement affects the construction.

For a garment with multiple components, specify which component receives the label. If the same item has several size variants, keep the size system consistent across the brief, size chart and label artwork.

## Treat packaging as part of the product brief

Packaging decisions may affect how garments are folded, protected, identified and prepared for the buyer's own distribution process. Share the desired fold, bagging approach, label or sticker requirements, barcode information if applicable, carton requirements and destination country.

Do not assume that a packaging option, special finish or branded material is available. Mark it for discussion and ask OSD to confirm it in the quote. Owner-sensitive options on the product drafts remain marked for review before publication.

## Build one approval checklist

Before approval, compare the following against the product brief:

- Brand artwork and spelling
- Size information and placement
- Care and fibre information supplied by the buyer
- Hang-tag copy, orientation and attachment
- Packaging instructions and identification needs
- A photograph or proof of the sample label and packaging where provided

A written approval record helps keep label and packaging changes from being confused with garment changes.

## Questions brands often ask

### Should labels be planned before sampling?

Yes. Even if final artwork is still being refined, label locations and information requirements can affect the sample and approval workflow.

### Can the same packaging work for every product?

Sometimes, but the product size, fabric, decoration and destination may change the practical requirements. Confirm the chosen approach for each product brief.

### What should I send with a quotation request?

Send the garment details, label and packaging artwork or references, quantities, destination country and any required product information. OSD confirms the applicable commercial details in the quote.
